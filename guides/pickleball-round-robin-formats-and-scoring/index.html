Pickleball round robin: formats, scoring and how to set one up

By the developer of Smash! Pickleball Manager · Updated 17 October 2026

The short answer: in a round robin everyone plays everyone. At a pickleball social that usually means one of three things: rotating partners (you change partner every round), fixed partners (teams stay together and play every other team) or singles. Pick the format, agree how matches are scored, and rank the table by points or wins.

The three formats

1. Rotating partners (a “mixer”)

You get a new partner and new opponents every round, and you are scored as an individual. It is the most social format and works well when skill levels differ, because everybody plays with everybody. It is also known as an Americano-style round robin. With 8 players you can make everybody partner everybody exactly once in 7 rounds: see the 8-player schedule.

2. Fixed partners

Pairs register together and stay together. With n teams, every team plays every other team: n − 1 rounds if n is even, or n rounds with one team resting each round if n is odd. The usual way to draw it is the “circle method”: fix one team in place and rotate the others around it. Six teams on three courts take five rounds:

3. Singles

One against one, same maths as fixed teams: with n players (n even) there are n − 1 rounds on n/2 courts. For an odd number, one player rests each round.

You don’t have to play every round. Stop earlier if time is short, as long as everyone has played the same number of matches.

Scoring: decide before the first serve

  • Race to a target. A normal game to 11 (or 15), win by 2. The most familiar, but games finish at different times, so courts drift out of step.
  • Fixed total. Play until the two scores add up to a set number (say 16 or 24). Every game then has the same length, which keeps the courts in sync. With a total of 24 a game might end 14–10.
  • Timed rounds. Play until the clock runs out and record the score. Simple to run, and everyone changes courts together.

Smash! supports a race to a target score and a fixed total, plus a round timer that beeps and vibrates when time is up.

Ranking the table

Most groups rank by points scored (or wins) and break ties with point difference (points for minus points against), then wins. Whatever you choose, say it out loud before the first round. Smash! ranks by points, then point difference, then wins.

Running the day

  1. Count the players, then pick a format from the table above. If the number doesn’t fit the courts, accept sit-outs and share them fairly.
  2. Number the courts and write the schedule where everybody can see it.
  3. Start each round together and call the changeover. A round timer saves arguments.
  4. Record scores immediately after each game.
  5. Add up the table, announce the winner, and keep the results to compare next week.

Odd numbers and late arrivals

Someone sitting out is normal, but a player who sits out earns nothing that round, so the table is only fair if everyone has played the same number of games. Smash! gives the next game to whoever has played least, lets late arrivals join mid-event and catch up, and has a Fair Ending option that projects missing games from a player’s average if you have to stop early.
